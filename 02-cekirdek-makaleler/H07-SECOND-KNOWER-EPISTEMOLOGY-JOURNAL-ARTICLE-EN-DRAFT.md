# The Second-Knower Thesis and Scientific Objectivity: A Critical Reconstruction of Zanabûn Epistemology

## Abstract

This article reconstructs and assesses the *second-knower thesis* in a historical formulation of Zanabûn epistemology. The primary text defines knowledge through a knowing subject's orientation toward a known object and treats the recognition of the resulting product by a second knower as crucial to the public status of science. The central question is whether such confirmation can ground scientific objectivity without reducing truth to agreement. Through conceptual analysis of the primary manuscript and comparison with philosophical discussions of scientific objectivity and methodological distinctions between reproducibility and replicability, the article proposes a four-level interpretation of second-knower confirmation: assent, critical assessment, reanalysis, and independent investigation. It argues that the thesis is most defensible when read as a requirement for accountable, evidence-responsive inquiry, rather than as a sufficient criterion of truth. This is a conceptual contribution and a research proposal, not a reported empirical result.

**Keywords:** Zanabûn; second knower; intersubjectivity; scientific objectivity; reproducibility; replicability; epistemology; philosophy of science.

## 1. Introduction

Scientific knowledge must be assessable beyond an individual investigator's private conviction. The historical Zanabûn account expresses this intuition through a relationship among a **knowing subject** (*bilen özne*), a **known object** (*bilinen nesne*), and a **second knower** (*ikinci bilen*). It presents knowledge as a product of directed inquiry and scientific standing as involving recognition or confirmation by another knowing subject (primary text, lines 10–12, 27, 113, 117).

The resulting philosophical problem is precise: **What kind of second-person confirmation contributes to scientific objectivity, and under what conditions?** Two observers can agree and still be wrong. Conversely, disagreement may prompt useful error correction. The task is therefore not to treat agreement as a truth-maker, but to specify what independent scrutiny adds to inquiry.

This article develops a charitable but critical reconstruction of that problem. It does not attempt to validate the manuscript's entire cosmology, theological ontology, or six-mode historical classification.

## 2. Materials and method

The method is **textual reconstruction and conceptual analysis**, not experimental science. The primary material is the original Turkish H07 manuscript, consulted alongside a line-aligned English working edition. The analysis follows four steps: (1) identify claims about subject, object, product, and second knower; (2) separate descriptive, normative, and theological claims; (3) compare the resulting thesis with established discussions of scientific objectivity and replication; and (4) propose an operational distinction among forms of confirmation.

No interviews, experiments, or numerical datasets were conducted or analyzed for this draft. The historical text is treated as the author's source of claims, not as independent evidence that the claims are scientifically true.

## 3. Reconstructing the second-knower thesis

The manuscript associates knowledge with the product arising when a knowing subject turns toward a known object (lines 12 and 111). Its recurring formulation says that science arises when the outcome of this orientation is confirmed by a second subject (lines 27 and 117). It also argues that a shared, independently existing reality is needed for knowers to encounter the same object; the second person's affirmation alone cannot substitute for the thing being investigated.

This yields three analytically separable propositions:

**P1 — directed inquiry.** A knowing subject investigates or acts upon a known object.

**P2 — publicly accessible result.** The activity produces a claim, observation, inference, or artifact that can be examined by someone other than the originating subject.

**P3 — second-knower assessment.** A second knower assesses that result in relation to the object and the relevant standards of inquiry.

P1–P3 are **editorial reconstructions** of the historical thesis, not quotations or a formally demonstrated theorem. The historical text also links science to truthfulness, goodness, love, and creative production. Those normative elements are important to its broader system but are not by themselves empirical conditions sufficient for scientific validity.

## 4. Objectivity: agreement is not enough

Accounts of scientific objectivity address questions of evidence, freedom from bias, and the role of communal criticism. The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry on scientific objectivity offers a framework for locating the social dimension of scientific judgment without treating mere consensus as infallible.

Consider two researchers who accept the same faulty calibration. Their agreement is genuine, but the result remains inaccurate. A second knower therefore contributes more when their assessment is **independent, methodologically explicit, and open to correction**. This strengthens the interpretation of the second-knower thesis as an epistemic practice rather than a binary vote.

The manuscript's broader inference from order to a first divine knower is a separate metaphysical argument. The conceptual point about independent checking neither proves nor disproves that theological premise.

## 5. Four levels of second-knower confirmation

To make the thesis analytically useful, this article proposes four distinct forms of confirmation:

| Level | Form of confirmation | What it can establish | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assent: a second person agrees | Shared belief or acceptance | Shared error remains possible |
| 2 | Critical assessment: a second person challenges reasoning and evidence | Exposes some logical and interpretive weaknesses | May depend on the same evidence base |
| 3 | Reanalysis: a second person reruns analysis on the same data and procedures | Tests computational or analytic reproducibility | Cannot by itself eliminate data-collection bias |
| 4 | Independent investigation: new data are collected to assess the claim | Tests whether a finding holds beyond one study | Still sensitive to design and systematic error |

This typology is an **editorial proposal**, not a four-stage classification supplied by the historical manuscript. It draws a methodological distinction informed by the National Academies' treatment of reproducibility and replicability (2019). The levels are not a guaranteed linear scale of truth: high-quality criticism at level 2 may reveal an error that a poorly designed level-4 study misses.

## 6. Creative knowledge and its limits

The source distinguishes the *science of being* from *creative science*. The former concerns beings and their interactions; the latter concerns humans deliberately generating new knowledge and artifacts from existing knowledge (lines 111–115). The second knower also appears here as someone who can assess or use what another has produced.

This suggests an important distinction between **successful use** and **scientific justification**. An artifact may be useful without its maker's explanation of why it works being correct. A work of art or a religious teaching may have genuine cultural and human value without being an empirically tested scientific theory. The source's broad inclusion of everyday, artistic, technical, and religious knowledge should thus be understood as part of its own expansive philosophical vocabulary, not as a standard contemporary taxonomy of scientific disciplines.

## 7. Objections and research implications

**Objection 1: circular confirmation.** If two knowers accept each other's assumptions, agreement adds little. **Response:** require explicit evidence and scrutiny independent of the initial claim where feasible.

**Objection 2: exclusion of solitary discoveries.** Can a claim discovered by one person be knowledge before another person checks it? **Response:** distinguish the possible truth of a discovery from its subsequent public scientific warrant. The second-knower thesis is more plausible as an account of accountable public inquiry than as a claim that unconfirmed truths cannot exist.

**Objection 3: conflation of ethics and validity.** Must the investigator be morally good for a claim to be scientifically true? **Response:** ethical conduct is important to trustworthy research practice, but the truth of a specific proposition is not settled by the investigator's virtue.

**Proposed future test.** A preregistered study could compare error detection among teams using assent-only review, adversarial methodological review, shared-data reanalysis, and independent data collection. This is a possible research design, not an experiment conducted for this article.

## 8. Conclusion

The second-knower thesis identifies a significant problem for epistemology: how inquiry moves from an individual's encounter with an object to a claim others can assess. Its defensible philosophical core is the requirement that knowledge claims remain answerable to independently inspectable reality and criticism. Agreement is relevant, but it is neither sufficient nor infallible. Interpreted in this restricted sense, the historical thesis provides a basis for a comparative discussion of objectivity, scientific practice, and creative knowledge, while its wider theological and cosmological assertions remain separate questions.

## 9. Expanded literature comparison and originality boundary (research revision, 22 October 2026)

### 9.1 Scientific objectivity and transformative criticism

The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry on scientific objectivity describes competing ideals: correspondence with facts, value freedom, freedom from personal bias, and the role of community practices. Its account of Helen Longino's critical contextual empiricism highlights avenues for criticism, shared standards, actual uptake of criticism, and tempered equality of intellectual authority. These conditions are **more demanding than the historical H07 wording that a second subject approves a result**. The present article should therefore avoid presenting intersubjective checking as a new discovery absent from existing philosophy of science. Rather, the candidate contribution is the reconstruction and critique of the distinctive Zanabûn subject–object–second-knower formulation.

**Literature source:** *Scientific Objectivity*, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-objectivity/ ; *The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge*, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-knowledge-social/ .

### 9.2 Testimony is not identical to independent verification

The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry *Epistemological Problems of Testimony* asks whether testimony generates or merely transmits knowledge, whether hearers need positive grounds for reliability, and whether justification depends partly on the speaker. This makes an important distinction for the H07 thesis: a second person who **believes a report** is not necessarily a second person who **independently verifies the object**. Both roles can matter, but their epistemic functions differ. The source's language of *onay* (approval/confirmation) does not by itself resolve which role is intended; a definitive interpretation requires authorial clarification.

**Literature source:** *Epistemological Problems of Testimony*, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/testimony-episprob/ .

### 9.4 Stronger objections to address before submission

1. **Prior-art objection:** Intersubjectivity and criticism already have extensive treatments. What does the Zanabûn framework add beyond new vocabulary? A defensible answer must identify a precise, nontrivial distinction or problem, not merely rename familiar concepts.
2. **Ambiguous confirmation:** Does *onay* mean assent, understanding, verification, moral approval, or practical usefulness? The historical source shifts among contexts; these meanings must be disambiguated rather than collapsed.
3. **Regress of confirmers:** If every result requires a second knower, must the second knower's judgment require a third? The article should distinguish public contestability from an infinite chain of actual approvals.
4. **Epistemic injustice:** Who counts as an authorized second knower, and can power relations suppress qualified criticism? Community criticism must be evaluated for access and response, not simply number of participants.
5. **Truth versus warrant:** A claim can be true before it is socially accepted, and widely accepted claims can be false. The thesis is strongest as a norm for **public warrant**, not as a metaphysical condition for truth.

## 10. Replies to objections and a delimited contribution (revision, 22 October 2026)

### 10.1 Response to the prior-art objection

The existence of mature traditions of intersubjective objectivity and social epistemology prevents a defensible claim that H07 discovered social checking as such. Its **candidate contribution** is instead a historical and conceptual reconstruction of a three-position relation: the initiating knower, the object investigated, and the second knower who assesses a resulting product. This is a contribution to interpretation and conceptual comparison, contingent on faithful translation and demonstrable differences from existing formulations. It is **not yet** an established novel theory of scientific justification.

### 10.2 Response to the ambiguity of *onay*

The Turkish term *onay* in the primary manuscript should not automatically be translated as laboratory verification. Depending on context, it may indicate assent, recognition, practical uptake, normative approval, or an attempt at evidential confirmation. The journal article should use **second-knower assessment** as a neutral umbrella term, and reserve **verification**, **replication**, and **reproduction** for explicitly specified methodological acts. Where the historical text appears to identify these senses, the article must report the ambiguity rather than erase it.

### 10.3 Response to infinite regress

The historical assertion that a second knower is required can generate a regress: if each approval itself requires another knower's approval, no result ever becomes warranted. A restricted interpretation avoids this by distinguishing **actual serial endorsements** from **public contestability**. A result can be provisionally warranted when it is available to qualified scrutiny under revisable standards; no endless chain of positive votes is required. This is the present article's **editorial proposal**, not a resolution explicitly supplied by the primary text. The original metaphysical claim about a first knower remains a separate question.

### 10.4 Response to unequal authority and epistemic exclusion

A second knower can be mistaken, coerced, excluded, or systematically privileged. Accordingly, counting confirmations cannot by itself measure epistemic reliability. Review practices should enable reasoned dissent, make evidence accessible where ethically and practically possible, document conflicts of interest, and respond to substantive criticism. These conditions draw on established social-epistemological discussions; they are not claims that the original H07 text already specified modern institutional safeguards.

### 10.5 Response to truth versus warrant

**Truth** concerns whether a proposition corresponds to the relevant state of affairs; **warrant** concerns what reasons or evidence justify accepting it. A true claim need not await another person's assent to become true. Conversely, two or more agreeing people can accept a false claim. The strongest academically defensible reading of the second-knower thesis concerns the **public assessment and defeasible warrant** of knowledge claims, rather than the creation of truth by consensus. This reading narrows the historical author's more expansive formulation and must be labeled as reconstruction.

### 10.6 Formalized thesis, counterexample, and research boundary

**Restricted thesis R:** *When an inquiry produces a publicly assessable claim about an independently existing object, methodologically competent and suitably independent second-person scrutiny can strengthen its public warrant by exposing errors that uncritical assent would leave undetected.*

R is **defeasible**, not a biconditional or a universal necessity claim. It does not imply that every truth needs two people, that two people suffice to establish truth, or that theological propositions become empirically testable through agreement. A simple counterexample to the unrestricted version is a pair of observers who agree because they use the same miscalibrated instrument. A counterexample to the claim that actual approval is necessary for truth is a correct result not yet reviewed by anyone else.

**Originality boundary:** R overlaps with existing accounts of criticism, testimony, and replication. The publishable question is whether a careful interpretation of Zanabûn supplies a distinctive conceptual articulation, historical perspective, or productive criticism of these accounts. That cannot be decided without further literature and primary-text review.

### Research questions for close reading

**Longino comparison.** Does the second-knower thesis specify conditions for transformative criticism, or merely require a second person's approval? If the latter, the novelty claim should be limited to its historical framing and its relation to the author's ontology.

**Goldman and Hardwig comparison.** Is the second knower an independent examiner, a competent witness, a user of a created artifact, or all three in different contexts? The original H07 source uses *onay* in more than one setting. A scholarly article must distinguish these roles and explain why each matters.

**Merton and Oreskes comparison.** Does a normative expectation of honesty, openness, and criticism justify a scientific claim? No simple inference is warranted; institutional trust and the evidential truth of particular findings must be analyzed separately.
